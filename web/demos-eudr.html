One plot against the EUDR cut-off

A cocoa plot in Côte d'Ivoire, given as one point. emem checks it against the forest baseline and loss year at the 31 December 2020 cut-off and returns a signed, Annex II shaped statement. This page checks the receipt and the facts behind the verdict.

writes
none by you; the responder may fetch and sign on a miss
checked
in this tab, with emem-verify-core.js
schema
/v1/schemas/eudr_dds.json

What you will see

  • A plot verdict with how well the inputs support it, and the limits the response lists.
  • The dataset versions actually read, and the TMF cross-check.
  • The receipt checked here, and each fact behind the first cell re-hashed here.
1statement

The statement for the plot

One call. A plot emem has read before answers in seconds. A new one can take minutes, and can fail with source_fetch_failed naming a slow tile; running it again usually reads more.


      

    

  


  

    
2receipt

    

      
The receipt covers every cell's facts

      
The receipt signs the union of the per-cell fact ids. This page checks the signature and that no cell's facts are missing from it.

      

    

  


  

    
3facts

    

      
The facts behind the first cell

      
Each fact id is the BLAKE3 hash of its canonical bytes. These are the ids an operator keeps in the Article 12 record, so each one is fetched and hashed here.

      

      

    

  


  

    
What this does not show

    
    
      
  • Legality under Article 9(1)(b), or forest degradation under Article 2(7). The response says so in legality_disclaimer and degradation_disclaimer, quoted in step 1.
    • 
      
  • The whole plot. A point samples one cell of about 9.5 m, for a plot of up to 4 ha; sampling_caveat says so.
    • 
      
  • A probability. verdict_support.level grades how complete the inputs are; it is not calibrated.
    • 
      
  • A TRACES NT reference. ddsReferenceNumber is an emem placeholder; TRACES NT issues the real one.
