One field: its edges and its pixels

A small box of farmland near Ludhiana. emem returns the field polygons inside it and two signed Sentinel-2 band grids. This page re-hashes the grid bytes, checks each receipt, and computes NDVI from the two grids itself.

writes
none by you; the responder may fetch and sign on a miss
checked
in this tab, with emem-verify-core.js
boundaries
Fields of The World, CC-BY-4.0

What you will see

  • Field polygons drawn over a true-colour picture of the same box.
  • Two native 10 m grids, s2.B08 and s2.B04, whose bytes hash to the name the signed record gives them.
  • NDVI per pixel, computed in this tab from those bytes.
1edges

The field boundaries in the box

The boundaries come from Fields of The World, read per map tile, so polygons run past the box. clean resolves overlapping and duplicate polygons and says what it changed.


      

    

  


  

    
2grids

    

      
Two signed band grids, re-hashed here

      
A band raster is a grid of values over the box from one pinned Sentinel-2 scene. The receipt signs the derivation, and the record names the grid's BLAKE3 hash. This page downloads the bytes and hashes them.

      

      

    

  


  

    
3NDVI

    

      
NDVI, computed in this tab

      
NDVI is (B08 - B04) / (B08 + B04) per pixel. It is computed here from the two grids you just checked, only when both come from the same scene. The result is not signed; the inputs are.

      

    

  


  

    
What this does not show

    
    
      
  • That a polygon is a legal parcel. Fields of The World is a model's map of field edges, not a land register.
    • 
      
  • total_area_m2 is over the map tiles read, not over your box. The count covers the tiles too.
    • 
      
  • That the picture is signed. /v1/scene.png is a view for people. It is a window of the same size centred on the box, so its edges can sit a pixel off the polygons.
    • 
      
  • Burn scars or water use. POST /v1/field_burn_scar and POST /v1/field_actual_et answer those for the same field; the skill walks both.
    •
