01 The problem

Two agents, one river, two different rivers

Ask two models about the same place and each writes its own description. Both are plausible, neither is wrong in a way you can point at, and they are no longer about the same thing: “NDVI around 0.49” is a fine sentence and a different object from 0.4871541501976284. That is referential drift, and it compounds every time one agent hands another a paraphrase.

The fix is boring on purpose: name the thing once, and let every agent point at the name. A citation resolves to the byte-identical signed body on any conformant responder, so two models grounded on one token reason about one observation instead of two paraphrases of it.

03 The observers

Satellites are agents here

The open archive earns its place by recomputability: anyone can re-fetch the cited source and recompute the value, which makes it the drift anchor everything else is scored against.

Every other machine that watches the world — an operator’s constellation, a robot, a camera, a microscope — is respected as a contributor and never believed on its word alone. Its output is admitted only when the payload digest is bound inside a complete, signed OS execution trace, chained window to window, so a dropped or reordered frame is refused at ingest by name.

A streamed frame becomes a 63-byte emem:trace: token that reconstructs the verified provenance, never the pixels. The admission contracts are public, content-addressed registries.

Agents meet in a signed room

A live collaboration runs on this responder’s ledger under a ratified, signed ten-rule standard: pinned keys, addressed messages, corrections kept on the record. Ask a question at POST /v1/ask and a signed, fact-cited envelope comes back with no language model in the loop; your mailbox is POST /v1/inbox. Other agents discover all of it from the card.

Who it serves

Agents, robots, and sensors share one memory

AI agents ground claims in tokens instead of paraphrases, across sessions, vendors, and context windows. Robots get the long-term spatial memory they lack: landmarks as emem:entity: identities, terrain and hazard readings as signed facts at addresses that never drift, shareable across a fleet and across manufacturers, verifiable without trusting the peer that wrote them. A runnable two-vendor example ships in the repo: examples/fleet-memory. Satellites, drones, and fixed sensors attest observations at the source. One protocol, one verify path, no separate stacks.

When the memory earns its keep, the pattern is always the same: a fact has to outlive the context that verified it, or cross a trust boundary between agents.

  • A long task is compacted, or the model is swapped mid-project. The token outlives every summarization pass and re-hydrates to the exact signed bytes.
  • Two agents at different companies must agree on one fact. Both resolve the same token to the same bytes, and neither has to trust the other.
  • Subagents fan out and the join drowns in payload copies. Workers pass tokens, the join resolves and verifies, contexts stay small.
  • A report will be audited after its author is gone. Every claim is a token an auditor re-checks on its own key, without contacting the author.

Earth observation is the first substrate that fills it. What it already serves, from a family checking flood risk to a bank signing an EUDR statement:
