Worlds: real places in 3‑D, built on signed facts

A world is a Gaussian-splat reconstruction of a real place that you can orbit and fly through. The picture is not the evidence: the measurements under it are emem facts you can resolve by token and check yourself.

01What a world is

Each splat in a world carries one of three tags, and the viewers can colour or hide splats by tag.

measuredsits on a signed elevation node, coloured from a Sentinel-2 scene
interpolatedfills the surface between measured nodes
synthesizedinvented texture (rock, water, fields) so the place reads as a place; marked and removable

A photoreal scene is cheap to generate and cannot be trusted by looking at it.

A world here is meant to be the opposite. Its measured layer points back to facts with a content address and a signature, so a person or an agent can check what the scene stands on instead of believing the pixels.

Mind the proportions. In the dense worlds the measured splats are a small share: 9,216 of 1,623,721 at Haridwar, about 0.6 percent. The rest is densification and texture.

So what you can verify is the measured base and the facts it came from, not the look of the whole scene.

02Sparse worlds: one splat, one cell

These are drawn by this page's own viewer. Each splat is one cell of signed facts at a fixed place on Earth, at most 1,024 cells per scene.

Click a splat to read its values and open its fact in /verify. The page hashes the scene file in your browser and shows whether its SHA-256 matches the baked metadata.

  • Grand Canyonelevation
  • Interlakenelevation, vegetation, water
  • Cairo and Gizaelevation, GeoTessera embedding
  • Rondôniabiomass, forest loss year

03Dense worlds: flyable scenes with a time series

Eight scenes built in a separate project and served from disk under /splats. They hold 91 thousand to 3.7 million splats each, and most carry a dated Sentinel-2 series you can scrub through.

Two viewers read the same files: the classic viewer and a newer one built on the Spark renderer. Spark also replays recorded agent runs and verifies them in the page.

Each world ships a token ledger, emem_provenance.json, listing the emem fact tokens its NDVI lineage stands on. The button on each card resolves those tokens against emem.dev now, from your browser.

The image on each card is the world's own Sentinel-2 input as served, input_s2.png, cropped to fit.

04What is unfinished

Both dense viewers are partly built. This is what a visitor will run into, as checked against the served files and emem.dev on 2026-09-29. The ledger buttons above re-run the token checks live.

  • The Pattan farm has no ledger.Its derivation is pending with the note "no resolvable NDVI facts at centre", so it lists no parent tokens. Its per-cell facts in the scene digest do resolve.
  • Soubré has one date.All 15 of its parent tokens resolve, but it has one Sentinel-2 date, so the timeline has nothing to scrub.
  • A manifest signature names no one.Each world's manifest is signed with a key derived from the manifest itself, so anyone holding the manifest can make the same signature. The viewers now say content-addressed rather than ed25519 signed. What a reader can check is the root cid and the chain to emem-signed facts, which the ledger buttons above do.
  • One overlay is missing.world_intel.png does not exist for Keylong and Soubré. The classic viewer asks for it and carries on without it.
  • The downloads are large.The classic viewer fetches a .splat of 2.9 MB (Soubré) to 119 MB (Grand Canyon). Spark streams a paged .rad first, but the big worlds are still tens of megabytes.
  • The Ask panel is model text.Both viewers can put a question to a local model over the scene's facts. What it writes is not a fact; only the fact ids it cites can be checked.

Fixed on 2026-09-29: every ledger's derived NDVI token and Keylong's cube and raster tokens resolve again (they had been left out of a storage move), Soubré's four missing parents resolve, the classic readout lists each band's fact with its own check, Soubré is in the classic menu, and Spark counts measured splats apart from the rest.

Re-derive: POST /v1/memory_token/resolve {"token": "<emem:fact:...>"} for each token in /splats/<world>/emem_provenance.json.

The measured points are signed facts. Each sits at a fixed cell on Earth and carries the signature of the key that recorded it. The other points are derived from them, and the panel on the left counts both.

Click a point to read its value, its band and the content address another agent can check. The satellite basemap underneath is reference imagery and is not signed.

Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom. Verify one yourself or see how the memory is built.
